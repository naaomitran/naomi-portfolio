nautical crime investigation services

Nautical Crime Investigation Services (NCIS) is a startup that specializes in leveraging cutting-edge technology to enhance national security and maritime defense. Using artificial intelligence and advanced tracking systems, NCIS conducts comprehensive risk assessments and detects criminal activities both at sea and on land.

background

Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA): Being aware of activities occurring in the marine domain that may have an impact on the security, safety, economy, and environment of the ocean.
Goal: Develop understanding and transparency of activities and threats at sea like people, infrastructure, cargo, vessels, and any other forms of transport on the waters and collect sufficient and accurate information of ongoing maritime activities to identify potential threats, make informed decisions, take appropriate action, share information with relevant partners, and respond to maritime activities by working with the government incorporating situational awareness to minimize resulting damages.

Automatic Identification System (AIS): Transmits a vessel’s position, showing its position to other vessels and organizations.
Limitations: Human analysis is needed to interpret AIS data, and it's normal and legal for vessels to turn off their AIS signal.

research scope

GRACE (MDA PLATFORM)

An AI powered risk assessment tool that uses machine learning to predict and identify suspicious activities and notify the appropriate organizations to intercept before it occurs and improve maritime security. Grace should be able to predict with high accuracy and certainty what and where suspicious activities occur and who is involved.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

We will identify the functional requirements and design requirements for the MDA platform, Grace, and recommend which design concepts and principles are essential to include for an MDA platform.

RESEARCH GOAL & QUESTIONS

Goal: To understand how existing MDA platforms are currently supporting their needs and how their user experience is like with these interfaces.

Questions:

  1. How helpful are the features / which features are helpful?
  2. What is displayed on the interface enough for effective and productive interpretation?

This will help us learn what features are necessary to include in Grace, provide insight on how we can improve user experience, and eliminate assumptions or misunderstanding in users’ context, needs, expectations, and abilities which can then minimize human error.

requirements


Grace should be usable by many different groups with varying responsibilities and interests

  • Government (monitoring, control, and surveillance (MCS) and enforcement agencies): ensure fishing vessels are following regulations and pursue those that are participating in illegal activities
  • Fisheries officers: enforce the federal and provincial regulations for protecting fish, wildlife, and natural resources
  • MCS workers: deter illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) activities relating to the sea
  • Maritime insurers: interested in the conditions of vessels and the safety of the people on the vessels, as they are liable for bodily injuries or deaths of a person, and the damage of the vessels
  • Seafood suppliers: interested in how their product is sourced
  • Maritime journalists and researchers: investigate and report news regarding the ocean, including ocean health, impacts of fishing and other ocean activities, and suspicious vessels and activities
  • NGOs and citizens: interested in a variety of topics, including seafood sustainability and suspicious activities at sea, and may make it their responsibility to report these findings to the respective authorities
  • Fishermen: aware of their location for transshipment and encounter purposes, or to prevent collisions

Grace should provide sufficient and effective features for each of these groups and their specific tasks to satisfy their functional requirement.

Geographic

Grace aims to be successful in underdeveloped countries, like the subregion of Africa. The interface design depends largely on cultural differences, as different cultures will have different challenges and opportunities, affecting how people perceive and interact with each other and other entities, like computer systems.


The design must incorporate user behaviours and values from these following areas and prioritize creating relatively universal features that are applicable to all end users, while maintaining its functional requirements and providing a good user experience to satisfy their design requirements.

experiment

Human need: To bring maritime domain awareness and detect and deter crimes.

Central tasks: To detect suspicious activity accurately and efficiently.

GLOBAL FISHING WATCH (GFW) VS SKYLIGHT (SK) (COMPETITIVE REVIEW)

This review was to familiarize myself with these MDA platforms to identify what worked and didn't work. I explored GFW's map directly and watched Skylight's tutorial videos. Strengths and weaknesses were noted down and then categorized to UX/UI specific or algorithmic qualities since we will only be focusing on UX/UI aspects. This data was used to create evaluation goals for our usability test:

  1. How helpful is the map / interface for finding vessels?
  2. How helpful are the filters in supporting their tasks?
  3. Is the available information displayed on the interface sufficient for effective interpretation?

USABILITY TEST (OBSERVATION & INTERVIEW)

We used this method to identify where we could improve MDA platforms in terms of usability and user satisfaction. Participants used GFW and SK and completed the following tasks:

  1. Can you find Long Wang Chyun NO37 in 2021 and tell me a bit about what you know about the vessel from the interface?
  2. Can you find a Spanish trawler fishing in a prohibited zone?
  3. Can you identify an encounter between 2 vessels and tell me what each of their flags are?
  4. Beside fishing related activities, can you identify any other crimes from this interface?

An interview followed the osbervation and consisted of 9 open-ended questions (see Appendix in report).

findings


Global Fishing Watch Qualitative Analysis

Strengths

  • Cues help with interpretation, learnability, and usability
  • Encourages discoverability / felt comfortable playing around with interface
  • Helpful features offered like the Search for vessels function

Weaknesses

  • Slow and heavy / overwhelming interface
  • Unclear cues and poor feedback results in misinterpretation
  • Confusion around using some of the features suggests it's not an intuitive interface
  • Users wished there was a feature that could inform them of more specific activities

Differences between SK & GFW

  • Visual hierarchy: SK's features are located in a toolbar vs GFW's features are in a side panel; SK has dropdown tabs vs GFW has a scrollable panel
  • Visual cues: SK complements their text with icons to indicate it's a button
  • Function/workflow: SK waits for users’ queries before loading anything on the interface, while GFW defaults to show everything they have collected from AIS and VMS

Quantitative Results & Analysis

See report for full results but overall, participants completed tasks quicker using Skylight. We cannot determine which MDA delivered more accurate results due to our small sample size.

designs

To improve the overall usability and user experience, we determined the requirements needed for Grace:

  1. Functional: detect / monitor vessels and predict suspicious activity with high certainty
  2. Usability: easily learned and retained
  3. User characteristics: globally understood
