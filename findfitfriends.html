case study

find fit friends

with Angel Bao, Will Oxtoby & Gordon Wu

problem

College makes it easy to meet people and stay active. Post-college, less proximity and busier schedules make both harder — especially during the stressful transition from studying into a full-time job.

solution

An app for finding friends with similar physical activity interests — working out, spin class, walking, hiking, and more. You request to be friends, then see each other's posts: photos, comments, and a schedule of when, where, and what's planned, like a social platform built around getting active together.

design direction

📱 dating app × social feed × calendar

👥 find friends to stay active and accountable with

🗓 make coordinating activities effortless

how I worked

role & approach

  • frame the problem Started from the gap itself — why staying active and staying connected gets harder right after college — before touching any UI.
  • ground it in real users Built out concrete scenarios (below) to stress-test whether the concept actually held up for the people it was meant to serve.
  • explore, then weigh trade-offs Designed two competing directions and evaluated each on pros and cons rather than picking a favorite on instinct — see lo-fi prototypes.
  • validate the assumption Ran an experiment to test the concept directly, and documented findings and limitations honestly rather than just the parts that worked.

grounding the concept

user scenarios

Sally has been in LA for a month as a software developer — her first job post-grad, and the transition has been stressful. Now more settled, she wants to get active and is eyeing a spin class, but doesn't know the area and doesn't want to go alone. She has no friends nearby yet, and would love someone to join her weekly — ideally someone close by so they could walk and explore the city together too. She's only free Tuesdays at 4PM or Saturday mornings before noon.
James and Hannah are new friends who both love tennis, and the Vancouver weather has finally cleared up. They don't have each other's numbers, so they need another channel to coordinate — and both dislike the usual back-and-forth of picking a time and place. Once it's settled, they each add it to their own calendar: James uses iCal, Hannah uses Google Calendar.

weighing the options

lo-fi prototypes

design alternative 1

Inspired by the familiar dating-app swipe pattern, with messaging and a scrolling feed modeled on standard social conventions.

pros: leans on patterns users already know

cons: scheduling isn't intuitive yet, and profile info reads as scattered — needs more design work

design alternative 2

Inspired by BeReal/Instagram — a scrollable photo feed, a search function to find people, and profiles that open into messaging. Schedule resembles a monthly calendar.

pros: more community-first — you can message anyone after viewing their profile, no friend request required

cons: the add/remove event buttons may go unused if people expect to edit events straight from the date itself
